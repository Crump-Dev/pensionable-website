Ben Gunnee to lead TPR's Market Oversight Division

18 February 2026 — The Pensions Regulator 5 min read

The role and what it covers

The Pensions Regulator announced on 18 February 2026 that Ben Gunnee will take up the leadership of its Market Oversight Division. The division sits at the commercial end of TPR's supervisory mandate — responsible for overseeing the largest and most systemically significant parts of the UK pensions landscape: master trusts, Independent Governance Committees (IGCs), and large occupational schemes.

Gunnee brings extensive regulatory experience to the role, having worked across financial services supervision and market conduct. His appointment signals a deliberate choice by TPR to place sector expertise at the head of what is arguably the regulator's most commercially sensitive division — one that engages directly with the major providers managing workplace savings for millions of members.

What the Market Oversight Division actually does

Market Oversight is TPR's mechanism for engaging with the structure of the pensions market itself, rather than individual scheme compliance. The division's remit breaks down into three principal areas.

Master trust supervision is the division's most prominent function. Since the introduction of the master trust authorisation regime under the Pension Schemes Act 2017, TPR has held ongoing supervisory responsibilities over authorised master trusts — verifying that they continue to meet the five authorisation criteria: fit and proper persons, systems and processes, scheme funder requirements, business planning, and continuity strategy. As master trusts have grown — some now administering tens of billions in assets — the supervisory burden has intensified correspondingly.

IGC oversight covers the independent governance committees that workplace pension providers are required to maintain. IGCs assess value for members of contract-based workplace pension products. TPR's Market Oversight function monitors whether IGCs are operating with genuine independence and whether their value assessments are rigorous. The FCA holds concurrent jurisdiction here, and the two regulators have deepened their coordination in recent years.

Large scheme engagement rounds out the division's work — supervising schemes above certain size thresholds with a more proactive, risk-based approach than the reactive compliance focus applied to smaller schemes.

What it means for master trusts specifically

Master trusts will feel the most direct effect of Gunnee's appointment. TPR has been sharpening its supervisory approach to master trusts through a programme of enhanced data collection, deeper financial resilience assessment, and closer scrutiny of scheme funder arrangements. The division under Gunnee is expected to continue this trajectory, with particular focus on:

  • Value for money: The joint DWP/FCA/TPR VFM framework, currently being finalised, will require master trusts to report on investment performance, costs, and service quality on a standardised basis. Market Oversight will be the division receiving and acting on that data.
  • Consolidation activity: The market has continued to consolidate, with smaller master trusts exiting or merging into larger providers. Gunnee's division oversees the wind-up and transfer processes that accompany such consolidations, which carry significant member protection obligations.
  • Systems and cyber resilience: TPR's supervisory expectations around operational resilience — including IT systems and data security — have grown significantly. Large master trusts administering critical national infrastructure in pensions terms are now subject to scrutiny that parallels financial services regulation.

Authorisation vs. ongoing supervision

Master trust authorisation is a one-time gate. Ongoing supervision by Market Oversight is continuous — covering financial sustainability, member data, continuity planning, and value delivery throughout a trust's operating life.

The oversight framework and what triggers scrutiny

TPR uses a risk-based supervisory model for market oversight. Schemes are categorised by risk profile, and those rated higher will receive more intensive engagement. The factors that tend to trigger closer attention include: deteriorating financial health of the scheme funder, scheme growth that outpaces administrative capacity, adverse member outcomes data, and weak governance structures identified during routine data submissions.

For IGCs specifically, TPR and the FCA have jointly issued guidance on what constitutes genuine value assessment. IGCs that produce formulaic assessments without evidenced analysis of actual member outcomes are increasingly in scope for regulatory scrutiny. Gunnee's oversight of this space will be watched closely by providers whose IGC arrangements have historically been less than robust.

Regulatory expectations going into 2026

The appointment lands at a busy moment for market oversight policy. The VFM framework is moving towards implementation, pensions dashboards are entering their final staging phase, and the government's Mansion House reforms continue to push pension capital towards productive finance. Each of these policy strands creates new supervisory demands on the division Gunnee will lead.

Schemes interacting with Market Oversight should expect continued emphasis on data quality and completeness — particularly around member benefit data for dashboards — and on demonstrable value delivery rather than process compliance. TPR has been explicit that it wants to see outcomes, not paperwork.

Key takeaways

  1. Ben Gunnee leads Market Oversight from February 2026 — the division covering master trusts, IGCs, and large scheme supervision.
  2. Master trusts face the most direct impact — expect continued scrutiny on VFM reporting, consolidation processes, and operational resilience.
  3. IGC oversight is tightening — surface-level value assessments will attract attention; genuine, evidenced member outcome analysis is the expectation.
  4. Data quality remains the watchword — dashboards readiness and accurate member records are core supervisory focuses heading into 2026.
  5. Risk-based supervision means not all schemes feel equal pressure — but deteriorating financials or governance gaps will accelerate engagement.
