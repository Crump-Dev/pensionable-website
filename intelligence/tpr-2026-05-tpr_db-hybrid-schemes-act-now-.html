DB and hybrid schemes urged to act now to get their value data ready for dashboards

14 May 2026 — The Pensions Regulator 5 min read

What pensions dashboards require from DB schemes

The pensions dashboards programme — the government-backed initiative to give members a consolidated view of all their pension savings in one place — has been in development for several years. From 2026, schemes are required to connect to the MoneyHelper pensions dashboard and make member data available through the ecosystem. TPR's 14 May guidance is directed specifically at DB and hybrid schemes, whose data requirements are structurally more complex than DC arrangements and where preparedness remains uneven across the sector.

At its core, dashboards require schemes to respond to member find requests — when a member searches for their pension through a connected dashboard, their scheme must return a matched record and, once identity is verified, present "value data" giving the member a meaningful indication of their benefit entitlement. For DC schemes this is relatively straightforward: accumulation pot values are calculated daily and can be returned almost automatically. For DB and hybrid schemes, the picture is considerably more involved.

The data quality challenge facing DB schemes

DB schemes hold member records that in some cases span decades of service, multiple scheme rule changes, benefit structures of varying complexity, and administration transfers between providers. The quality of the underlying data varies dramatically — not just between schemes, but within them. Historic records may carry missing dates of birth, incorrect National Insurance numbers, incomplete pensionable pay histories, or unresolved deferred member records that predate modern administration practices.

TPR's annual survey of scheme data scores has consistently found that DB schemes rate their own data below the standards required for reliable dashboard responses. The gap between a data score adequate for annual benefit statement production and the data quality required for automated, real-time dashboard matching is material — and often larger than scheme administrators initially expect when they begin the remediation process.

⚠
The data quality gap is real

TPR research consistently finds that DB schemes underestimate the effort required to achieve dashboard-ready data quality. Schemes that have not yet conducted a formal data audit should treat that as an immediate priority — the gap between current state and required standard is almost always larger than anticipated.

What "value data" means in practice

For DB schemes, value data presented to members through a dashboard must include an accrued-to-date pension amount — representing what the member has earned so far, expressed as an annual income from normal pension date. For deferred members, this is their preserved benefit, potentially with revaluation applied. For active members, it is their earned pension as of the most recent calculation date.

Hybrid schemes — which contain both DB and DC elements within the same arrangement — face the added complexity of presenting both types of value data accurately. The DB component requires the calculations described above; the DC component requires a current fund value. Ensuring that both sets of data are accurate, current, and presented in a consistent format for the dashboard connection requires administrative capability and governance oversight that many hybrid scheme administrators are still building.

The data standards set by the Pensions Dashboards Programme specify exact formats for how benefit data must be structured and transmitted. Schemes must not only have the right numbers, but must be able to present them in machine-readable form through a compliant administration software or dashboard connection provider. This technical integration layer adds another dimension to the preparation challenge.

Staging deadlines and the consequences of non-compliance

MaPS (the Money and Pensions Service) has established a staging timeline that brings schemes into dashboard connection in waves, based primarily on scheme size. The largest schemes — those with 10,000 or more members — were required to connect earliest, with progressively smaller schemes staged through to 2026 and beyond. DB and hybrid schemes should confirm their specific staging deadline with their administrator or via the DWP guidance tool, as individual staging dates vary.

Schemes that miss their staging deadline without a deferral in place face regulatory action by TPR. Available powers include compliance notices, improvement notices, and ultimately civil penalty notices. More significantly, members who cannot find their pension through a dashboard because the scheme has not connected will be unable to make informed consolidation or retirement planning decisions — a harm that TPR has indicated it will treat seriously.

"Pensions dashboards are one of the most data-intensive obligations the industry has faced. The administration work required is not trivial — but the member benefit, and the regulatory consequence of inaction, are both real."

What to do now

DB and hybrid schemes that have not yet begun their dashboard preparation should prioritise three actions immediately. First, conduct a formal data audit to establish a baseline data score and identify specific gaps in member records — particularly around historic deferred members and records transferred in from previous administrators. Second, confirm the scheme's staging deadline and build a project plan that works backwards from that date, accounting for the time required to remediate data gaps, test the dashboard connection, and complete staff training.

Third, engage with the scheme's administration provider to confirm their dashboard connection capability and the timeline for technical integration. Not all administration systems are equally advanced in their dashboard readiness, and schemes may find that their provider's technical timeline constrains their own preparation schedule. Early engagement avoids discovering this constraint too late to address it.

Key takeaways

  1. DB and hybrid schemes face structurally harder dashboard preparation than DC — benefit complexity and historic data quality gaps are the primary obstacles.
  2. Value data means accrued-to-date benefit amounts in machine-readable format — not simply the ability to produce a benefit statement on request.
  3. Data audits almost always reveal larger gaps than expected — begin remediation early, before the staging deadline creates time pressure.
  4. Staging deadlines are legally binding — missing them without a deferral in place exposes schemes to TPR compliance and penalty notices.
  5. Technical integration with a dashboard connection provider takes time — engage your administrator now to confirm their readiness timeline.
