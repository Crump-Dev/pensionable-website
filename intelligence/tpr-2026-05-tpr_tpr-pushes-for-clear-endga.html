TPR pushes for clear endgame planning as DB schemes remain in surplus

06 May 2026 — The Pensions Regulator 5 min read

The surplus moment

The defined benefit landscape looks, on paper, better than it has for a generation. Aggregate funding across UK private sector DB schemes reached levels not seen since the pre-financial crisis era, with the combined surplus of schemes in the PPF 7800 index persistently above £200 billion across 2024 and into 2025. The rapid rise in gilt yields from 2022 onwards dramatically reduced the present value of pension liabilities, while asset portfolios — particularly those with LDI strategies that survived the September 2022 gilt crisis — largely kept pace.

But aggregate surplus figures mask significant variation. A minority of schemes remain materially underfunded, with deficit recovery plans still running. The majority are funded well enough to consider their eventual endgame seriously. It is that majority that TPR's May 2026 guidance targets. The regulator's message is pointed: being in surplus does not remove the obligation to have a plan. If anything, a well-funded position is the optimal moment to exercise that choice — while the power to negotiate from a position of strength remains intact.

Three endgame routes

TPR frames the endgame decision around three principal routes, each with distinct characteristics, costs, and member outcome implications. Understanding all three is the prerequisite for any credible endgame strategy.

Buyout transfers all scheme liabilities to an insurance company in exchange for a premium. Members become policyholders of the insurer, and the sponsoring employer's pension obligation is extinguished. Buyout provides the highest level of member security — policies are backed by Prudential Regulation Authority-regulated insurers, ring-fenced from the sponsoring employer's business — and has historically been the dominant endgame route. The cost is real: insurers price in a risk margin and profit loading, which means a scheme typically needs to be more than 100% funded on a buyout basis to transact without additional employer cash. However, for schemes that have reached full funding on a buyout basis, the case for acting promptly is strong.

Run-on means the scheme continues under trustee governance indefinitely, investing assets to generate returns and paying benefits as they fall due. Run-on became a more credible option following the Mansion House reforms, which explicitly affirmed it as a legitimate long-term strategy and created the conditions for schemes running on to invest in productive UK assets. The surplus extraction provisions in the Pension Schemes Act 2021 and subsequent DWP work on the surplus sharing framework allow trustees and employers, in certain circumstances, to return surplus to employers — providing a commercial rationale for well-funded schemes to remain open as investment vehicles. The risk of run-on is longevity and investment: if the scheme underperforms or members live longer than projected, the surplus can erode, creating a funding gap that the employer must plug.

Superfund consolidation transfers liabilities to a commercial consolidator — a pension superfund authorised by TPR — rather than an insurer. Superfunds accept schemes at a lower price than full insurance buyout, pooling longevity and investment risk across a larger portfolio of schemes. The gateway criteria for superfund transfer have evolved since the Clara-Pensions model launched, and TPR's updated guidance provides clearer tests for when a superfund transfer is appropriate: broadly, where full buyout is not immediately achievable and there is a stronger member outcome case for superfund than for scheme run-on under the existing sponsor.

"A scheme in healthy surplus without a documented endgame is a governance gap. The funding window TPR and trustees have worked towards for years is now open — and windows do not stay open indefinitely."

Buyout market capacity

The bulk annuity market has faced capacity constraints in recent years, driven by the simultaneous surge in schemes reaching buyout-ready funding levels. Insurer capacity — measured in terms of actuarial resource, reinsurance availability, and capital allocation — has not grown at the same pace as demand. The result has been a queuing dynamic: schemes that have engaged insurers early, maintained clean data, and prepared professional run-off benefit specifications are transacting. Those that approach the market cold face delays, and in some cases find that the insurer pricing premium exceeds the scheme's current surplus, creating a funding shortfall that must be addressed before buyout can proceed.

This market dynamic makes preparation time-sensitive. TPR's guidance notes that trustees should begin insurer engagement well before a buyout is expected to be affordable, using the interim period to prepare scheme data, resolve benefit specification uncertainties, and develop trusted relationships with transaction advisers. Schemes that wait until they are technically affordable may find themselves at the back of a queue, with pricing that reflects the insurer's elevated demand rather than the scheme's ideal transaction terms.

What "clear plan" means to TPR

TPR is not prescribing which endgame route schemes must take. It is requiring that trustees have considered the options, reached a reasoned decision, documented the rationale, and built a credible pathway toward their chosen destination. A "clear plan" in TPR's language involves several practical components: a current funding assessment on both technical provisions and buyout bases; a projection of when various endgame milestones are expected to be reached; a risk management strategy to protect funding levels in the interim; and an explicit discussion of the member outcomes implications of each route considered.

The plan should be reviewed regularly — at least triennially in connection with the actuarial valuation cycle, and more frequently if market conditions change materially. It should also be accessible to TPR on request. Trustees who cannot produce such a document are increasingly exposed not just to regulatory scrutiny but to the practical risk of a deteriorating funding position eroding choices that are currently available.

Steps to endgame readiness

For schemes that have not yet formalised their endgame thinking, the starting point is a trustee-level strategy session facilitated by the scheme actuary and investment adviser. This session should review the current funding position, stress-test projections under a range of market scenarios, and give trustees a clear picture of which endgame options are currently within reach and on what timeline. Many trustees find that a formal strategy review crystallises the decision quickly — the analysis often shows that buyout is closer than informally assumed, particularly for smaller schemes where the premium-to-asset ratio is more manageable.

Data readiness is the operational precondition for all endgame routes. Insurers, superfunds, and run-off administrators all require clean, complete membership data — accurate benefit specifications, correct accrual histories, up-to-date addresses, and resolved GMP equalisation positions. Schemes with known data quality issues should initiate a data improvement programme as a priority, regardless of which endgame route they ultimately choose. A well-documented data cleansing exercise also reduces the risk of post-transaction disputes and litigation.

Key takeaways

  • Aggregate DB surpluses remain historically high — the funding window for endgame action is open now
  • The three main routes are buyout, run-on, and superfund transfer — each with distinct cost and risk profiles
  • Bulk annuity market capacity is constrained; early insurer engagement is essential for well-funded schemes targeting buyout
  • TPR expects a documented, board-owned endgame plan — not merely a preference expressed in passing
  • Data quality underpins all routes; GMP reconciliation and benefit specification work should begin immediately
  • Schemes without a plan are a governance gap — and risk losing the favourable conditions currently available
