Richard Knox appointed as Executive Director, Strategy, Policy and Analysis

15 January 2026 — The Pensions Regulator 5 min read

The function and what it does

TPR announced on 15 January 2026 the appointment of Richard Knox as Executive Director for Strategy, Policy and Analysis. The SPA function is where TPR's regulatory thinking is formed before it reaches the industry as consultation, guidance, or enforcement action. It occupies a central place in the regulator's architecture — translating broad policy objectives from government and Parliament into the specific regulatory frameworks that trustees, sponsors, and administrators work within.

The Strategy, Policy and Analysis directorate encompasses several distinct capabilities: the development of TPR's own corporate strategy; policy analysis and the formulation of TPR's responses to legislative change; economic and actuarial research; and the analytical infrastructure that underpins risk-based supervision across the wider organisation. In short, if a new regulatory expectation is coming, it will have passed through this function first.

Why this appointment matters for schemes

For those responsible for tracking regulatory direction — whether scheme actuaries, governance consultants, or in-house pensions managers — the SPA function is the canary in the mine. The individuals who shape TPR's thinking here determine which issues get elevated to enforcement priorities and which remain guidance-level expectations. Richard Knox's background and priorities will influence the texture of TPR communications for years.

The appointment is particularly significant given the volume of live policy work in progress. DB endgame regulation following the Pension Schemes Act 2021 funding code, the joint VFM framework for DC schemes, pensions dashboards implementation, and the government's Mansion House productive finance agenda are all simultaneously active. Each requires TPR to have a coherent analytical position. The SPA directorate supplies that foundation.

TPR's 2026 strategy priorities

TPR's published corporate plan for 2025–2028 sets out the strategic themes that the SPA directorate will be working to advance. Several are directly material to scheme governance teams:

  • Ensuring savers get value: The VFM framework sits at the centre of TPR's DC agenda. Knox's function will shape how value metrics are defined, measured, and enforced — a process that will determine competitive dynamics across the master trust market.
  • DB funding and endgame: The new DB funding regime, effective from September 2024 for new valuations, is producing its first wave of post-code funding statements. SPA analysis of the data will inform whether TPR's interventions are well-calibrated or require adjustment.
  • Pensions dashboards: The analytical work behind dashboard staging deadlines, data standards, and compliance expectations flows through SPA. Schemes uncertain about their obligations should track this function's outputs closely.
  • Future of work and auto-enrolment: With the extension of auto-enrolment thresholds under active policy consideration, SPA analysis shapes how TPR will administer an expanding compliance population.

How policy translates to scheme obligation

SPA produces TPR's consultation responses, research papers, and analytical frameworks. These documents typically precede formal guidance or code amendments by 12–18 months — making them early-warning signals worth tracking.

How schemes can stay ahead of regulatory direction

The most effective way for scheme governance teams to track TPR's policy trajectory is to engage systematically with the regulator's research and consultation outputs, rather than waiting for final guidance. TPR publishes an annual corporate plan, interim reports, and thematic reviews throughout the year. Each is a product of the SPA function, and each carries signals about where supervisory focus is heading.

Schemes should also pay attention to TPR's speeches and industry conference appearances, which often serve as informal advance notice of forthcoming policy positions. When TPR's Chief Executive or directors begin discussing a topic repeatedly, formal guidance typically follows within a year. With Knox now in post, watching the SPA team's research outputs is the highest-value intelligence activity available to governance professionals.

Finally, the SPA function drives TPR's engagement with government on legislative proposals. Schemes that respond substantively to DWP consultations on pensions legislation are, in effect, feeding into the policy environment that SPA will later translate into regulatory expectation. Active participation in the consultation process remains the single most direct lever available to the industry.

Key takeaways

  1. Richard Knox leads TPR's strategy brain from January 2026 — this function sets the analytical foundation for all regulatory developments.
  2. Five active policy streams run through SPA simultaneously: VFM, DB funding, dashboards, auto-enrolment, and productive finance.
  3. SPA outputs are early signals — research papers and consultation responses typically precede formal guidance by 12–18 months.
  4. Tracking TPR speeches and thematic reviews is the most time-efficient way to anticipate supervisory direction shifts.
  5. Engaging with DWP consultations directly shapes the policy environment SPA will later translate into regulatory requirements.
